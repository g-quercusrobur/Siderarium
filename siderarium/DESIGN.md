# siderarium — system book

A cosmic clock. It renders the sky of the moment as an instrument: the frame has the discipline of a watch dial, the content is the trip.
Every mark on the dial is computed from the sky by a stated rule. Nothing is ornament.

---

## 1 · Principles

1. **Austere frame, maximal content.** Grid, type and interface are strict and colourless. Everything inside the circle is computed light.
2. **One variable, one channel.** No astrological factor drives more than one visual channel, and no channel takes more than one factor.
3. **One authority per channel.** Golden Dawn decides colour. Itten's circle and Albers' interaction decide colour harmony; the dial discipline of haute horlogerie (chapter ring, applied numerals, the Moon in its aperture, nothing that does not read) decides structure. Lilly decides dignity.
4. **Traceable.** Any mark can be explained from the readout. The readout is part of the product.
5. **Earned excess.** Stars, mandalas and portals appear only when the sky produces them.

**Dignity (Lilly).** domicile +5 · exaltation +4 · triplicity +3 (Ptolemaic day/night) · term +2 (Egyptian) · face +1 (Chaldean) · detriment −5 · fall −4 · peregrine −5 · mutual reception lifts peregrine (+5). Outer planets: none.

## 4 · The instrument

- **Container.** A dodecagon, Saturn's black, edged in lead. Twelve sides hold every Ptolemaic aspect as a vertex subset (2, 3, 4, 6, 12). Quintiles and septiles do not fit it and look foreign against it, which is correct.
- **Houses.** Vertices are cusps and faces are houses: ascendant on the left vertex, MC on the top vertex. The frame is fixed to the observer's horizon. It does not turn.
- **Rete.** A monochrome chapter ring: degree ticks and sign glyphs in off-white, rotated so the ascendant degree sits on the eastern horizon. Planet notches in their tinctures mark true longitude. Sign colour never appears on the frame; it lives in the field and the auras.
- **Sky.** Planets are placed in their real local sky. The angle is the Placidian mundane position, the same unequal division as the planetary hours: rising on the left, culminating at the top, setting on the right. The radius is the altitude. The noon sun stands high in summer and low in winter, as on the Prague Orloj. Below the horizon the sun is switched off.
- **Portal.** The inner disc, half the sky's radius, centred on the pole. Mandalas live here and only here.
- **Pole.** The axis. Coloured by the day ruler and the hour ruler.

## 5 · Mandalas

An aspect is a fraction of the circle: angle = k·360/n → the star polygon {n/k}.

| aspect | {n/k} | figure |
|---|---|---|
| conjunction | 1 | rose of P petals, P = sum of the two planets' numbers (sun 6 + venus 7 = 13) |
| opposition | {2/1} | vesica piscis on the axis |
| trine | {3/1} | triangle |
| square | {4/1} | square |
| sextile | {6/1} | hexagram, seed of life when soft |
| quintile / biquintile | {5/1} {5/2} | pentagon / pentagram |
| septiles | {7/1} {7/2} {7/3} | heptagons |
| semisquare / sesquisquare | {8/1} {8/3} | octagons |
| quincunx | {12/5} | twelve-point star |

**Hardness.** Soft aspects bow their edges into petals and add a circle chain through the pole. Hard aspects keep straight edges with points at the vertices. Benefics (venus, jupiter) soften; malefics (mars, saturn, pluto, uranus) sharpen.

**Rose engine.** The primary figure is wrapped in a guilloché band: 5 to 9 passes of r(θ) = R·(1 − a + a·f(Pθ)), where f morphs from a cosine (round petals) to a cusp (spikes) with hardness. A second band at 2P, counter-phased, makes the moiré.

**Two colours.** The lines carry the planets, the fill carries the relationship. Planet A's rose-engine passes twist clockwise in A's colour and planet B's twist counter-clockwise in B's, so the engraving crosses as two forces. Every star edge passes from one planet's colour to the other's: smoothly when soft, with a hard split at the midpoint when hard. Circle chains alternate. White appears only when a planet demands it (neptune's brilliance, uranus's pale iridescence).

**Colour law for the fill (Itten chords).**
- soft, fusion, creative: **light**. Additive, interpolated in OKLab.
- hard: **pigment**. Multiplicative in linear RGB, chroma pulled down. Mud.
- anything mixed with saturn goes black. Black figures are drawn as grooves carved into the light, edged by the other planet's colour.
- pluto lends no pigment; it bends the space instead.

**Orientation.** Vertex zero points at the first planet's true longitude on the rete. Rotation comes from the sky, never from a design choice.

**Constellations.** Figures share the portal at the same radius. Only where vertices of two figures coincide: a white star with a diffraction cross. Plain edge crossings do not count (tested: they read as beads). Major stars are joined by a hairline: the figure of the day. Two centred, aligned figures share gcd(n₁, n₂) vertices.

## 5b · Size, cut, guardians

- **Size.** Jupiter owns scale and Saturn owns restriction. e = 0.3·(jupiter score − saturn score)/10, plus 0.6 if jupiter is in the figure, minus 0.6 if saturn is, plus 0.15 for venus or sun, minus 0.3 for pluto. Scale = 1 + 0.45e, clamped to 0.5–1.5. When jupiter slows, the expansion is halved: it turns inward. All figures share the portal radius, so constellations still form.
- **Cut.** The strongest hard aspect (square, opposition, quincunx, semi- or sesquisquare) from a third planet to exactly one planet of the figure removes a wedge. The wedge points at the afflicting planet, its width is 24–100° by power, and its edges are hairlines in the afflictor's colour. The moon never cuts.
- **Guardians.** The day ruler and the hour ruler as their planetary stars: saturn triangle, jupiter square, mars pentagram, sun hexagram, venus heptagram {7/3}, mercury octagram {8/3}, moon enneagram {9/4}. They stand point up, inside a soft light; the pole is no longer a point. One ruler (the first hour of every day) gives one pure figure; two rulers overlap and share gcd(n₁, n₂) points.

| planet | primitive | when slow or retrograde |
|---|---|---|
| mercury | rays, spokes, radial lines | outgoing strokes smear |
| venus | curves: petals, circle chains, the Harris weave | curves soften |
| mars | points and straight star edges | points dull |

| saturn | the container's inner edge | the rim turns porous |
| uranus | iridescence | slows |
| neptune | global diffusion | **inverted**: diffusion withdraws, the field sharpens |
| pluto | lensing | weakens |

Blur is full while retrograde and ramps in as a direct planet slows toward its station, so the shadow period is visible.

## 6b · State rings

Every planet wears a ring whose form is its essential condition: whole for domicile, doubled for exaltation, thin for minor dignity (triplicity, term, face), faint for peregrine, broken into segments for detriment (exile), broken and drawn in for fall. The colour is the sign, purity follows the dignity score, retrograde blurs it, combust bleaches it. Outer planets wear the same forms in their optics: uranus iridescent, neptune white, pluto a hairline.

## 6c · Ripeness: the moon's electional condition

Shown at the centre, where the rulers of the hour already sit, and stated in the caption.

- **next aspect** = the first Ptolemaic aspect the moon will perfect with a classical planet before leaving its sign. None = **void of course**.
- **afflicted**: applying to mars or saturn by conjunction, square or opposition. **under the beams** within 12° of the sun, **combust** within 8.5°. **via combusta** 15° libra – 15° scorpio.
- **helped**: applying to venus or jupiter, or applying by trine or sextile.

| verdict | centre |
|---|---|
| ripe | the light burns warm and bright |
| open | normal light |
| unripe | the light dims and turns cold, the guardians fade |
| void | the light goes out: a dark pupil, the guardians as ghost hairlines. the door is closed |

Caption, right column: `moon applying square saturn · 37%` or `moon void of course · 28%`. The planetary day begins at sunrise, so before dawn the caption can read "saturday · day of venus". That is correct.

## 7 · Hierarchy

power = (0.2 + 0.8 · rarity) × aspect weight × (0.3 · exactness^1.5 + 0.7 · window) × modifiers

- **rarity** = log of the pair's synodic period, from the moon (a month) to neptune–pluto (492 years), normalized.
- **window** is measured in time, not degrees: exp(−½(days to exact / 1.5)²). Slow pairs linger in orb for years. They open the portal only in the days around an exact pass.
- **aspect weights** conjunction 1.0 · opposition .9 · square .8 · trine .7 · sextile .5 · quincunx .35 · minor .26–.32
- **modifiers** applying ×1.2, separating ×0.8, station ×1.5, sun involved ×1.2, angular ×1.3
- **events override:** eclipse > lunation > cazimi > outer-planet exact > station
- **the moon takes full part.** Its windows are hours (0.25 days), not days, so it rises only near exactness. It may cut the figure, but only by aspects it is applying to (hard aspects, or conjunction with mars or saturn). Flicker is accepted: it is true.
- one primary figure, at most two secondary figures. Below 0.25 the primary shrinks: a quiet sky.

## 8 · Field

A colour field, not a texture. Three lights over midnight: the ascendant from the eastern horizon, the sun and the moon at their real positions, each in its sign colour and weighted by sect. Below the horizon the field is slightly darker: under the earth. No noise, no smoke, no photographs. Every structure inside the circle is computed geometry.

Rejected and why: a noise nebula (encodes nothing) and a planetary interference field (nine summed wave sources look like random blobs, so it reads as noise anyway). Both remain reachable for comparison with `?field=smoke`.

**Harris weave.** One figure spanning the two luminaries: the circles through both sun and moon, drawn only between them, as a spindle of arcs passing from gold to silver. Projective geometry in the manner of Frieda Harris's Thoth backgrounds, computed from where the two actually stand. At new moon it closes into rings; at full moon it spans the dial. The sun's prismatic rays were removed: from the viewer's side they read as "some planets emit a grid".

## 9 · Type and grid

- **Geist** (OFL, self-hosted) for all text, the readout and the wordmark (wide tracking); **Geist Mono** only for typed inputs and the chart legend. One family, no third-party request.
- Ordinary capitals: names, days, places and the start of every line. The wordmark alone stays lowercase. Houses in Roman numerals, in the text face.
- **The field is the engraving; guilloché is an option.** Under the menu's field setting, grain d'orge is drawn as fine as the engraving's wobble: rings 4.4 u apart, each a wave of 96 lobes and 1.25 u of amplitude, alternate rings half a period apart so the lines close into small barleycorn lenses; line weight still carries the light as before. Never finer than 3 px between rings or 1 px of wave, so a phone does not turn it to moiré. `?field=waves` keeps the older engraving, `?field=glow` the glow field.
- Every export, square or wallpaper, carries the notice: © year Siderarium · proprietary design and method · all rights reserved.
- The interface owns one colour, off-white `#ebe7dc` at three opacities. All other colour belongs to the sky.
- The square master is 2048 px. The dial is centred at 45% of the height. The caption band sits on a hairline at the bottom, with 6.25% margins.
- Motion is clockwork: ticks and steady rotation, no bounce.

## 13 · Round 5 changes

- **One projection.** Every body sits at its ecliptic longitude on the same angle as the rete, with the ascendant on the eastern horizon; its distance from the centre is its altitude. (Earlier the bodies used a time-based house projection while the ring used longitude, so a planet could sit beside the wrong sign glyph. The data were right; the drawing contradicted itself.)
- **Equal houses** from the ascendant. With the ascendant fixed on the left, the regular dodecagon *is* the equal-house frame. The MC is drawn where it actually falls, as a pointer across the container.
- **Planets as polygons.** Core = the planet's own polygon (saturn 3 … mercury 8), point up. Ring = the same polygon, larger, whose form shows the condition (§6b) and whose rotation shows the alignment: aligned for domicile and exaltation, a quarter-step off for minor dignity, half off for peregrine, a full half-step (vertex against edge) for detriment and fall. Size grows with power (dignity, angularity, station, part in the figure), up to about 1.6×.
- **Contrast.** Darker ground, stronger lights, solid line cores, a deeper vignette. Portal base radius 0.56 R.
- **The luminaries' field.** The weave is the flow of a source (sun) into a sink (moon): in the plane, a source–sink pair's streamlines are exactly the circles through both points, and the equipotentials are the Apollonian circles. Particles run along the streamlines from gold to silver; their pace follows the moon's light. Rings around the moon draw the cup. At new moon source and sink coincide and nothing flows.
- **Chord.** Each planet of the figure sounds the note of the sign it stands in (Paul Foster Case's colour–note scale: the King-scale colour wheel read as the chromatic scale, aries = C). Three or more notes forming a known chord are named (C7, G-, Gmaj7…); otherwise the figure's two planets give a dyad, first > second. Aspects become intervals: opposition = tritone, square = minor third, trine = major third, sextile = whole tone.
- **Sigil layer.** Veils the sky and draws the ruling figure, its cut and the guardian stars in solid ink, for copying.
- **The next 24 hours.** A strip under the dial plots three windows, from the three mother letters: ideation (aleph, air), incarnation (mem, water: descent into form), destruction (shin, fire). Each window combines the figure's planets and signs, the figure's nature, and the beauty of the moment (whole, harmonious guardians, ripe moon; destruction takes the opposite). A slider previews any minute in the next 24 hours, and the next peak of each window over the coming week is listed: tap to go there.
- **Chart mode.** No field. Signs as full sectors in their colours, equal houses, the meridian drawn, aspects in their own colours (opposition red, square orange, trine blue, sextile teal, quincunx green dashed, minor aspects dotted), and leader lines from each planet's exact degree to its glyph.
- **Next chord shift.** The caption's right column ends with `next chord F#ø · fri 16 oct 16:23`. The engine walks forward in 10-minute steps until the chord's name changes, then halves the interval to the minute. It looks a week ahead and takes well under a second.
- **Chord, revised.** The chord is the sky's harmony: the notes of the two strongest figures, moon included, weighed by the sky's own weight (no angularity: the turning horizon changed the ranking every few hours and the chord with it). Measured over two weeks: about 2.7 changes a day, median hold almost 5 hours, a named chord about a third of the time.
- **Motion.** Running, the clock advances with the real frame interval and redraws every frame the screen gives. Blurs and the eclipse search are skipped and the heavy readings (readout, week scan, next chord) wait until it rests. Speeds: 15 min, 1 h, 4 h, 24 h of sky per second. The run button becomes pause. Choosing a window stops the clock on that moment.
- **Place.** On the first visit the dial takes the city of the device's own time zone at once (no permission needed), then asks for the device's position and uses it if granted. "here" asks again. The city menu holds one city for each time zone, west to east, with its current offset.
- **Listen.** A button sounds the chord as a slow organ drone: sine partials 1–4 for each note (pure tone plus overtones, the organ pipe), each doubled with a ±0.12 Hz detune so it breathes. The root sounds again an octave below. A = 440: the 432 Hz "cosmic tuning" is avoided on purpose. Swell 3 s, low-pass at 2.2 kHz, a generated 3-second reverb. While on, the drone glides to the next chord when the sky shifts, so the harmony moves audibly while the clock runs.
- **Round 7.** Font: Geist and Geist Mono (one family; the wordmark is Geist with wide tracking). Caption and frame type scale up on small screens; exports keep the master size. The date field is typed in the place's own time (yyyy-mm-dd hh:mm). Turning the dial travels in time: clockwise is forward, one turn is one day, a wheel notch is ten minutes. The readout uses full words. An about section gives the concept, history, methods and references. Outer planets carry no essential dignity. Every planet within 17° of the sun is under the beams. Sigil lines thinner. An engraved field (light as line weight, no glows) is available as a prototype behind `?engrave=1`.
- **Zoom.** Two fingers pinch and pan; ctrl/⌘ + wheel (or a trackpad pinch) zooms at the pointer; + and − keys; double click or double tap returns to the whole dial. Up to 8×. The plate is redrawn at the zoomed scale, so lines stay sharp. One finger and the plain wheel still move time. Engraving lines 20% coarser, so the engraving shows at screen size.

## 15 · Round 9 changes

- **Houses: Regiomontanus**, Lilly's system. The equator is divided into twelve from the meridian and each division is carried to the ecliptic by a great circle through the north and south points of the horizon; such a circle is the horizon of a latitude whose tangent is tan(φ)·sin(H), so each cusp is an ascendant computed for that pole. The MC is the cusp of the 10th, where astrologers expect it. The regular dodecagon stays as the container; the cusps are drawn across its band where they fall, the angles stronger, the number mid-house. Equal houses and Placidus remain behind `?houses=equal` and `?houses=placidus`. The MC itself still sits where the sky puts it on a uniform zodiac: putting it at the top would bend the zodiac and the figures with it.
- **Conjunctions are one form.** Bodies joined by a conjunction aspect are grouped; the group is drawn closed on its common centre until the rings overlap, the rings fuse into a single outline (stroked on a scratch plate, interiors erased), the solid bodies fill as one material mixed from their tinctures by power, and the glyphs stand side by side outside the form in zodiacal order. Optics (Uranus, Neptune, Pluto) keep their own effects inside the fused ring. At a cazimi the Sun's hexagon simply swallows the planet.
- **Pluto occults.** The magnifying lens is gone. Inside Pluto's circle (its own state ring) the field is put out and only the structure remains, as under an eclipse; at the limb a corona of streamers in the colour sampled from what it covers, lit by its power. No magnification, no pigment of its own. In the figure, the figure's heart goes into eclipse; at a station, a corona rings the whole portal.
- **Void Moon:** the pupil at the pole is near black and the whole portal dims around it; the guardians' star fades to a ghost.
- **Wallpaper:** no wheel. The field, and the engraving, run to the edges of a portrait canvas the size of the phone; the dial is sized to the width and set below the lock-screen clock. No caption.
- **Prototype, not built:** `?bodies=luminous` draws the planets as lights instead of bodies: flat tincture, glowing by power, no sheen or shadow. For comparison against the metallic default.

## 16 · Round 10 changes

- **Planets as lights** is now the default: flat tincture with a hairline edge, glowing by power. The metallic bodies remain as a setting.
- **Menu.** The top bar is a wordmark, a segmented control (clock · chart · natal · transits) and two buttons, readout and menu. The menu holds view (sigil, listen, screen, about), settings (language, houses, field, planets), the nativity (summary, edit, save as, seen from) and the saved charts. "Synastry" is now called transits, which is what the mode computes.
- **Saved charts.** Named nativities are kept on the device (`cw.charts`): save as, rename inline, delete with a confirm, load. No account: nothing leaves the phone. Sync and a licence key, if ever, come later.
- **Relocation.** "Seen from · current place" recasts the nativity for the horizon of the current place: same instant, new angles and houses. The caption says "relocated to".
- **Out of bounds.** A planet whose declination exceeds the obliquity is flagged in the readout and wears a dotted fence beyond its ring.
- **Retrogrades.** Pluto retrograde: the occultation's limb and corona lose focus. Mercury slow or retrograde: the caption prints with a ghost offset, like a misregistered plate, and the readout gets a faint second impression. Mars retrograde dulls the points, Venus the curves, as before.

## The export file: a contract

`siderarium-charts-<date>.json` carries the saved charts between devices and addresses. Every future version of Siderarium must read every file ever written, so the format is a contract:

- Top level: `app` = "siderarium", `format` (integer, starts at 1), `exported` (ISO instant), `natal` (the loaded chart or null), `charts` (array), `settings` (object, optional, advisory).
- A chart: `id` (string), `name` (string, optional), `date` (ISO 8601 instant in UTC, the one field that cannot be lost), `place` {`name`, `label` (optional), `lat`, `lon`, `tz` (IANA zone)}, `unknownTime` (boolean). Nothing else is required; everything the app shows is recomputed from `date`, `lat`, `lon` and `tz`.
- Readers accept any `format` ≥ 1, read only the fields they know, ignore the rest, never require a field added later (new fields are optional with defaults), and never rename or change the meaning of a field listed here. A chart that lacks `id` is given one; a chart that lacks `date` or `place` is skipped, not fatal.
- The report (`siderarium-report-<name>.md`) is plain Markdown for reading; it is not an import format.

## The harmonic rose (the Synastry dial)

The pair's dial has no planets. Overlaid, two charts were noise, and the reading lives in the contacts, so each contact is drawn as a curve instead.

- **Harmonic per aspect, after John Addey:** conjunction 1, opposition 2, trine 3, square 4, sextile 6, quincunx 12. Each contact is a raised-cosine petal curve, `((1 + cos n(θ − θA)) / 2)^(1.5 + 0.75n)`. It is turned to the first chart's point θA, so its petals also reach the second chart's point. The exponent sharpens the petals as the harmonic rises.
- **Two summed layers:** one for harmony (each contact's `h`) and one for strife (its `s`), normalised to the strongest direction of the two together. The radius is `R0 + (R1 − R0)·√v`, so area grows with weight. Harmony is blue `#7fa4e0` and strife red `#ff5a2a`, blended with screen over a dark veil. Beneath them run up to 18 single contacts, faint, in the blend of their two signs' colours.
- **Dropped in pair mode:** the bodies, the second chart's ring, the contact lines, the composite figure, the stations, the planet optics and the planet notches on the zodiac ring. The houses, the zodiac, the field and the guardians at the pole stay.
- **Sigil:** the two outlines in ink, with strife as the lighter line.
- **Reading:** a "the rose" section gives the leading symmetries and their share of the weight, for example "three-fold, from trines (35%)". It also appears in the copied report and has a glossary entry. The composite chart is still read in the text but no longer drawn.
- The Lissajous runner-up was previewed (curves of the two chord roots in just intonation) and not adopted. It reduces a pair to one interval of twelve, and the many pairs whose chords share a root would all get the same ellipse.

## Algol

A switch in the Bodies table, under Algol's own row (its degree, its house and the next eclipse), is remembered between visits and shows Algol (beta Persei, the Demon Star) on the art. It appears on the clock, in the sky by its altitude like the planets, and on the chart and natal views just inside the zodiac, by its longitude.

- **Position:** the J2000 coordinates (RA 3h08m10s, Dec +40°57′20″) are carried to the true ecliptic and the horizon of the moment, giving 26°33′ Taurus in 2026.
- **The mark:** a black point with a hair of pale green-white light at its rim. A shadow around it eats the field, and a cold corona with seven drifting tendrils breathes slowly.
- **The eclipse:** the star's real eclipse uses the GCVS elements, minimum at JD 2445641.5135 with a period of 2.8673043 days. The aura widens and darkens as a Gaussian of the hours from minimum (σ 2.2 h). When it passes 0.3, the label reads "Algol · in eclipse". The period has drifted slightly since the epoch, so the minima are good to roughly an hour.
- **Exports and sigil:** exported images carry the mark; the sigil layer leaves it out.
- **Glossary:** has an Algol entry.


The Bodies table also lists the four angles after the planets, in house order: ASC (I), IC (IV), DSC (VII), MC (X). A chart without a birth time has none.

## Stone of the day (Transits view)

The Transits caption carries one line just before the chord: "Stone of the day: X or Y". On the desktop canvas it sits in the right column, level with the chord. The reading has a section with the reason.

- **One stone per day.** The rule is read at the place's local noon of the day on show (`stoneOfTheDay` in app.js, cached per chart, place and civil date), so it holds from midnight to midnight and does not change with the hour.
- **The planet** (`SD.engine.stoneOfDay`): the day's leading transit to the chart decides, with the Moon's transits left out because they pass within hours and a floor of 0.1 on power.
  - A hard transit from Mars, Saturn or Pluto is answered by the planet that tempers it: Jupiter for Saturn and Pluto, Venus for Mars.
  - Any other hard transit is answered by the planet of the chart it strikes.
  - An easy transit is carried by its own planet.
  - With nothing in orb, or no classical planet in the transit, the planet of the weekday.
- **The stones** (`C.STONES_KNOWN`): the owner's list of 51 stones, with 19 common additions. Nine additions are traditional: pearl, bloodstone, aquamarine, red coral, zircon, green, brown and polychrome jasper, iolite. Ten are common staples: black obsidian, lava stone, howlite, unakite, sodalite, rhodonite, rhodochrosite, lepidolite, celestite, chrysocolla. Each planet lists first the stones the tradition gives it, by the strength of the witness, then the Vedic, then the modern by fame, the owner's before the additions.
  - The traditions weighed: Agrippa I.23-29 (and I.32, II.14), Ficino III, Lilly's Stones lines (he copies Agrippa, who copies Ficino: one chain), and the Picatrix II.10 and III.1 apart; the Vedic navaratna (Phaladeepika II.29, Jataka Parijata II.21) and its widely agreed substitutes; for stones the tradition does not name, the modern attributions, Cunningham (1988) first.
  - Old names read as modern stones: chrysolite as peridot, the carbuncle as garnet, the heliotrope as bloodstone, the beryl as aquamarine, the golden marcasite as pyrite, the selenites as moonstone and selenite. Marked uncertain: the hyacinth (zircon, perhaps sapphire), Agrippa's Martial blood-stone (haematite or heliotrope), the eye of the Sun (tiger's eye by description), Proclus's helitis (sunstone by name).
  - A stone sits under up to three planets when the traditions give it strongly to each: emerald (Mercury, Venus, Jupiter), peridot, amethyst, diamond, lodestone.
  - Researched by three readers (the Western texts on disk; the Vedic sources; modern practice), synthesised, then checked by two adversarial verifiers. Their corrections are in: the Picatrix's iargonza is ruby, so it is no witness for zircon; Ficino reads feyrizech as sapphire, so he is no second witness for turquoise; the slot-fillers without a source were removed or moved to their attested planet.
- **The draw.** Each stone carries one element, the instrument's device [SD]. Among the planet's stones of the chart's leading element, one is drawn for the day by a hash of the date, the chart and the planet: the same chart and day always draw the same stones. The list's order weighs the draw (weights n, n-1 ... 1), so the traditional stones come up most often and every stone comes up in time. An element with fewer than three of the planet's stones is topped up from the chart's next element, its own stones still first, so a lone stone never owns a chart. The alternative is a second stone from the same pool, so the two do the same work for the day; it first followed another element, which for the Sun (eleven fire stones and peridot) gave peridot every day to every fire-led chart. A simulation over two years and the 24 orders of the elements reaches all 70 stones.

## Aspects in three colours (reading)

Every aspect written in the reading carries a colour: positive (green), tense (orange) or afflicting (red). A key sits under the Hierarchy heading.

- **The rule** (`SD.engine.aspectTone`):
  - Trine, sextile and the small creative aspects are positive.
  - Square and opposition are afflicting when Mars, Saturn or Pluto is in them, tense otherwise.
  - Quincunx, semisquare and sesquisquare are tense.
  - A conjunction goes by its planets: afflicting with an infortune, tense when a fortune (Venus, Jupiter) meets the infortune, positive with a fortune alone; within 8°30′ of the Sun afflicting (burnt), within 17′ positive (in the Sun's heart); otherwise uncoloured.
  - Septiles stay uncoloured.
  - The ranking of aspects and the infortunes are the tradition's (Ptolemy, Tetrabiblos I.13; Lilly, Christian Astrology); the reduction to three colours is the instrument's.
- **Where:** the figure, the hierarchy, the Moon's next aspect, the planets on the angles, the transits and the figures they close, the natal aspect cards. In a pair each contact is coloured by its own polarity in the rules v2.1: harmony share ≥ 0.6 positive, ≤ 0.4 afflicting with an infortune in it, otherwise tense.
- **The colours** were measured on black. Pure red is dim there: 5.3:1, and 4.2:1 to a protanope. The red is a coral, `#ff6b61`, at 7.5:1 (6.6:1 to a protanope). The orange is `#ffb454` (11.9:1). The green leans to teal, `#5fd3a1` (11.3:1), which keeps red and green apart under red-green colour blindness (ΔE 21 protan, 36 deutan, against 18 and 26 with the app's own green).

## Fix: a transit conjunct an angle

A transit by conjunction to the chart's Ascendant or Midheaven, when it was among the three leading transits, threw in `drawFigure` (an angle has no planetary number) and left the Transits dial blank. `numOf` now returns 0 for an angle.

## Planetary talismans timing

At the foot of the Clock's reading: for each of the seven planets, the next moments at the clock's place to make its talisman, as a date, a time window and its length. Each date is a link that opens the clock at that moment (the planet on the cusp) and starts its sound. The tap is the gesture that lets the audio begin.

**Sources.** Researchers read the primary texts and checked them against each other. The texts were Agrippa's Occult Philosophy in the 1651 English and the 1533 Latin, the Picatrix (Greer and Warnock, Attrell and Porreca, the Latin of Pingree), Thabit's De imaginibus, Ficino's Three Books on Life (Kaske and Clark), the Speculum astronomiae, Sahl, Dorotheus (Pingree), Bonatti's Considerations, Lilly's Christian Astrology and Ramesey. The 249 rules extracted are in the session's research files; the modern practitioners' corpus was not completed.

**The formula** is the one the image recipes repeat: "in the hour of the planet, the planet ascending" in its own sign or exaltation (Agrippa II.38-44; Picatrix II.10). The Midheaven is admitted as Agrippa II.30 and Picatrix III.7 admit it. "On the angle" means from 7° before the cusp, still to rise or culminate, to 3° past it. Houses use Lilly's five-degree rule.

**Core rules.** A moment that breaks any of them is never listed. The sources call these indispensable, give them as the timing of a harmful work, or say they turn the talisman against its maker (Agrippa II.22: made with the planet unfortunate, the table does "the contrary"; Picatrix I.4: the Moon aspected by Saturn or Mars is the timing "in omni opere malo").
- the planet's own hour;
- essential dignity, and not in detriment or fall (Agrippa II.34, its "dejections"; Thabit ch. 5; Lilly's table);
- direct, not combust, not cazimi ("all Planets are afraid of the conjunction of the Sun", Agrippa II.29);
- on the Ascendant (east of the meridian) or the Midheaven. Beyond the polar circles the formula's Ascendant is, for some hours of each sidereal day, the point setting in the west; such moments are refused;
- no conjunction, square or opposition from Mars or Saturn, applying, or separating within half the orb; between Saturn and Mars, the whole orb ("the worst of all is his square with Mars", Picatrix III.7). Orbs are Lilly's moieties, half of each planet's orb added together (his larger set, Christian Astrology p. 107: Saturn 10, Jupiter 12, Mars 7½, Sun 17, Venus 8, Mercury 7, Moon 12½), not the clock's narrower display orbs;
- not besieged between the bodies of Mars and Saturn;
- no Mars or Saturn (other than the planet) in the 1st house or within 5° of an angle (Picatrix I.4: in the ascendant it "will overturn and destroy the effect");
- the lord of the Ascendant neither retrograde ("nullo modo", Picatrix II.3) nor combust, nor in the 8th, nor in conjunction, square or opposition with Mars or Saturn without reception by domicile or exaltation. Thabit (ch. 1) and the Speculum (ch. 11) give that configuration for the images of expulsion and destruction, and "the contrary" for good ones;
- the Moon waxing ("nec lumine minuatur", Agrippa II.30), at least 12° from the Sun (Picatrix I.4), out of the Via Combusta (taken from 8° Libra to 15° Scorpio, the Picatrix's English bounds and Bonatti's and Lilly's together), not void of course, not within 5° of a node ("non sit in ecliptica", Agrippa II.30; Sahl 30, "horribile");
- the Moon neither joined to Mars or Saturn nor in their square or opposition, applying or within half the orb, the talisman's own planet no exception; her next aspect a trine, sextile or conjunction, and not to the body of the Sun, Mars or Saturn;
- the Moon directed: her next aspect to the planet itself (Agrippa II.29, "nihil enim sine lunae beneficio perfeceris") or to a fortune ("et iungatur fortunis bonis aspectibus", Picatrix I.4), or at the least her beholding a fortune and the lord of the Ascendant by trine or sextile (Picatrix II.3's minimum);
- no solar or umbral lunar eclipse from three days before to one day after (Bonatti's bound for the Moon's; kept for the Sun's [SD]).

The planet's day is weighed, not required. Agrippa II.29 asks for "day and hour", but II.34 allows "both or one", Picatrix II.10 names the hour only, and Ficino III.2 asks for the day "si fieri potest".

**Lesser impediments.** Each costs 6 to 14 points and is named in orange on the line. A moment carries two at most; three, never [SD]. They come in three kinds:
- *The Moon's misfortunes in the Picatrix's own list (I.4):* slow (under 12° a day), at the end of her sign (its last term), cadent in the 9th, in trine or sextile with Mars or Saturn. These are borne only with the remedy: Jupiter or Venus in the 1st or 10th, direct, out of the beams, out of its detriment and fall, and free of any aspect of Mars and Saturn (Picatrix I.4, "they will rectify an unfortunate Moon"; Ficino III.6, one of them is enough; Dorotheus V.5.11-13 on why they must be free). The talisman's own planet counts when it is the fortune rising or culminating.
- *The Picatrix's other "roots" (II.3):* Mars or Saturn in the 4th, 7th or 10th; the lord of the Ascendant cadent or in its detriment or fall; the Moon on the Ascendant (except for her own talisman; the Picatrix itself disagrees, I.4). These are broken only while II.3's minimum holds: the Moon beholding a fortune and the lord of the Ascendant by trine or sextile.
- *The other authors' (Sahl, Bonatti, Dorotheus, Ramesey):* the Moon in Scorpio or Capricorn, in the 6th, 8th or 12th, within 12° of a Node, within 12° of the full, her lord retrograde, burnt, or debilitated and cadent; the Dragon's Tail on the Ascendant or Midheaven; the Sun in the 1st outside Leo and Aries.

The line names the planet behind each: "Saturn in an angle", "the Moon in trine with Mars", "Mercury, lord of the Ascendant, cadent", "Moon in Capricorn, her detriment". A window lists every impediment met anywhere inside it, not only at its best instant.

**Sect in doubt.** A lesser election whose only dignity is a triplicity is not listed in the minutes when the Sun's rim is up and its centre is not, or the reverse. The sect is in doubt there, and the dignity with it [SD].

**Calibration.** Applied all at once and to the letter, every condition from every author leaves no moment at all: Rome had no Sun election in seven years. The sources expect this and rank their own conditions. Picatrix II.3 gives a minimum "si omnia ista non possent plene considerari", and I.4 and Ficino III.6 give the remedy for a necessary work. The rules above follow that ranking: what is harmful is excluded; what the sources call ideal is kept where its own author allows it to be broken. Measured by switching each rule off in turn and counting what it alone blocks. Even so, elections that keep every rule are rare: about one a year per planet at a given place, fewer far north.

**Tiers.** First-rate means the planet in its own sign or exaltation. Its own terms or triplicity (Agrippa II.30; the Picatrix III.7 fallback, with its conditions: in a masculine quarter, the 10th to the 12th house or the 4th to the 6th after Bonatti, so on the Midheaven before it culminates and on the Ascendant once risen; and, for Mars, Jupiter and Saturn, oriental) is listed apart, and only while the first-rate moments are more than 400 days off. Face alone is not accepted.

**Ranking.** Among the moments that pass, these rank higher:
- the planet's day as well as its hour;
- the Ascendant over the Midheaven for the first rate, the Midheaven for the lesser (Picatrix III.7), and the nearness to the cusp;
- the Moon applying to the planet itself, then to a fortune, then to the lord of the Ascendant; her beholding a fortune and the lord;
- her light and speed, her dispositor angular;
- the planet free of the beams, oriental for the superiors, occidental for Venus and Mercury, out of the terms of Mars and Saturn, out of their trine and sextile (Bonatti Tr. VII: "it subtracts something of its goodness"); the Moon out of their terms (Ficino III.6);
- the fortunes aspecting the planet; the lord of the Ascendant dignified and angular; the remedy present.

Within a window of 45 to 90 days from the first find, the three highest are shown in time order.

**The search** (`js/talismans.js`) runs in the page's idle time, 8 ms at a slice. Each moment is judged by `SD.engine.compute`, the same state the clock shows, so the clock opened at a listed moment shows its conditions. The steps:
- the planetary day is found from sunrise to sunrise, as the clock finds it;
- the planet's hours are scanned for the minutes it stands on the Ascendant or Midheaven;
- each window is judged at its best instant. When what fails there may lift as the sky turns (a house, an angle, the lord of the rising sign, the Moon perfecting an aspect), the window is searched six minutes at a time for an instant that passes;
- from that instant the window is walked outward two minutes at a time, so a condition that fails and recovers inside it is never stepped over. The edge is then found to a second or two;
- the Moon's next aspect is counted until its last second. In the minute before an exact aspect the clock's own reading has already moved on, the two disagree, and the moment is not listed;
- a planetary day is accepted only if it holds the instant asked for. An eclipse search that fails leaves those days unlisted;
- days when the Moon wanes from start to end are passed over before any chart is cast; a planet's dignity is read at both ends of the day, so an ingress inside it is not lost; the search starts a day early, so the planetary day under way is searched too (west of Greenwich it was being skipped);
- the engine keeps the planetary days it has found, so the many instants of one day share one set of sunrise and sunset searches. Each search starts a few seconds past the last crossing: begun on the very instant, it could return that crossing.

Slow planets step over the days or months without dignity. The full search takes about 8 to 11 seconds of computation per place, and the result is kept in `cw.talismans` for the day, the place and the house system. Horizons: Sun 3650 days; Moon, Mercury, Venus and Mars 2600; Jupiter 4600; Saturn 6000. When a planet has no dignity for a long time, the list says until when.

**Display rules:**
- A planet's dates appear only once its own search is finished; until then it reads "reading the coming years…". A half-searched list is not yet the best three, and its lesser moments can still drop out.
- A planet whose search fails shows that it failed and lists nothing. The other planets go on, and a run with any failure is not kept, so the next visit searches again.
- The window is shown in whole minutes inside its true bounds: the start rounded up, the end rounded down. The length given is the shown span.
- The link opens the instant the planet stands nearest the cusp, held inside the shown window.
- When a listed moment has passed, the search runs again from the present.
- Each line gives the Moon's mansion. Its fitness depends on the work, which the instrument does not know (Agrippa II.33).

**Cautions shown.** For Mars and Saturn, Ficino's rule: place them in their dignities only for one whose birth chart they signify, otherwise in their debilities (Three Books on Life III.10), and "when you fear Mars, set Venus opposite; when you fear Saturn, use Jupiter" (III.6). For all, the note to look at the moment against the maker's own birth chart, and the entertainment and study framing.

**Not computed:** the fitness of the Moon's mansion to the work (the mansion is shown), the dark and pitted degrees, the Part of Fortune, the lord of the preceding lunation, and the maker's nativity.

**Verification.** Three independent checks ran on the module: a recomputation of the listed elections from the astronomy library alone, an audit of every rule against the primary texts, and a line-by-line code review. Their findings shaped the rules above: Lilly's orbs, the sect, the Moon's own-planet exemption removed, the lord of the Ascendant, the Picatrix's gates, the polar Ascendant, the first day west of Greenwich.
